African Violets 101: Care Guide

A short guide to growing African violets (Saintpaulia, now classed as Streptocarpus) at home in South Africa.

Light

African violets need bright, indirect light, never full sun. Under LED grow lights, give 8–12 hours of light a day, with the tubes 20–30 cm above small plants and 30–40 cm above mature plants. Plants also need at least 8 hours of darkness each day.

Temperature, humidity and air

Aim for 18–21 °C at night, rising 3–6 degrees by day. Growth slows if temperatures stay below 15 °C for long. Humidity of 40–50% is ideal. Gentle, fresh air circulation helps prevent disease, but keep plants out of draughts.

Watering

Watering is the most important part of African violet care. Use room-temperature water. Let chlorinated tap water stand overnight, and avoid softened water. Wait until the top of the soil is dry to the touch, then water thoroughly. Water from the top at least every third watering, to flush out built-up salts.

Soil and feeding

Use a light, airy mix of peat moss and perlite, sometimes with vermiculite. Feed at one quarter of the manufacturer’s recommended strength at every watering, and only when the soil is already damp. Do not overfeed.

Pests and diseases

  • Mites are the worst pest. Check plants regularly.
  • Root-knot nematodes are avoided by using sterile potting soil.
  • New plants: keep them apart from your collection for 6–8 weeks.
  • Powdery mildew is prevented by good airflow. A light spray of about 100 ml full-cream milk in 2 litres of water can help.
  • Crown rot is avoided by not overwatering.

Propagating from leaves

Leaves are the easiest way to grow new plants. You can order leaves from us to start your own plants. See our leaf propagation page for video guides.

Leaf and flower types

Leaf types: boy, girl, ovate, pointed, round, heart-shaped, longifolia, bustle, quilted, strawberry, ruffled, wavy, holly, scalloped, serrated and spooned.

Flower types: single, semi-double, double, star, bell, wasp, tubular, fluted, ruffled, frilled, fringed, Geneva, bordered, bi-colour, multi-colour, fantasy and chimera.
